## Requirements

This course is part of the module _International Economics_, which also comprises a course in _International Finance_ as well as a seminar. Two-thirds of the module grade will be based on a written exam covering the two courses. For the seminar you will write a short term paper accounting for the remaining one-third of your module grade.

## Course Description

This course thoroughly examines the theoretical foundations and practical aspects of international trade. It examines why countries trade, what they trade, and how trade affects economic welfare, income distribution, and policy choices. Drawing on both classical and modern trade theories, as well as empirical evidence, the course equips students with the tools to analyze trade patterns, trade policy, and the implications of globalization.

## Course Outline

1\. Theories of International Trade

• Ricardian Model

• Specific-Factors Model

• Heckscher–Ohlin Model

2\. Trade and Income Distribution

• Effects on wages, capital, and inequality

• Empirical applications

3\. International Factor Mobility

• Migration and labor markets

• Foreign direct investment (FDI)

4\. New Trade Models

• Increasing returns and monopolistic competition

• Intra-industry trade and regional agreements

• Heterogeneous firms

5\. Offshoring and Global Value Chains

• Trade in tasks and wage effects

6\. Trade Policy

• Tariffs, quotas, and subsidies

• Trade policy under perfect and imperfect competition

## Course Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

• Understand and apply classical and modern theories of international trade

• Analyze the gains and redistributive effects of trade

• Examine the role of scale economies, imperfect competition, and firm heterogeneity

• Evaluate trade policy instruments such as tariffs, quotas, and subsidies

• Interpret empirical evidence and real-world applications of trade theories

• Understand political economy considerations in trade policy
