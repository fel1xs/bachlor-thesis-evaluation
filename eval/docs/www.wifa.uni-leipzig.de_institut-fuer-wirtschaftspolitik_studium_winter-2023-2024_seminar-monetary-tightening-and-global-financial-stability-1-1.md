## Seminar Topics

**Challenges to International Trade: Policies, Firms, and Technologies in a Shifting Global Economy**

Over the past decade, international trade has been reshaped by rising protectionism, the resurgence of tariffs, the strategic use of industrial subsidies, and the expanding influence of non-tariff barriers. Alongside these policy-driven developments, technological disruption and firm-level networks have added new complexity to global trade dynamics. Artificial intelligence is transforming how trade happens—and who benefits—while new research reveals that trade flows and business cycle comovement may be rooted not just in national policies, but in the decisions of globally connected firms. This seminar explores these interrelated challenges, asking how politics, firm behavior, and technology are reshaping the rules and structure of international trade.

| --- | --- |
| 1. | **Political Implications of Trade**<br>Globalization produces local winners and losers, that can shift voting behaviour and party coalitions.<br>To what extent do local trade shocks drive polarization and party realignment, and which policies could mitigate these effects?<br>_Autor, D., Dorn, D., Hanson, G., & Majlesi, K. (2020): Importing Political Polarization? AER P&P._ |
| 2. | **Policy Uncertainty and the Dynamics of International Trade**<br>Policy uncertainty can raise costs for firms by disrupting supply chains, deterring foreign investment, and making long-term planning more risky.<br>How does policy uncertainty affect trade and long-term competitiveness?<br>_Handley, K., & Limão, N. (2017): Policy Uncertainty, Trade, and Welfare. AER._ |
| 3. | **Industrial Policy and Trade**<br>Industrial subsidies can be perceived as unfair trade distortions, but they are often justified in terms of efficiency or innovation. <br>Do industrial subsidies reflect political capture more than productivity goals and how does it affect trade?<br>_Juhász, R., & Lane, N. (2024): The Political Economy of Industrial Policy. Journal of Economic Perspectives._ |
| 4. | **Subsidy Spillovers and Retaliation**<br>Governments’ increasing use of subsidies has surged, influencing both exports and imports, while producing heterogeneous effects across countries and, in some cases, fueling trade tensions.<br>How does the design of subsidy policies explain differences in trade effects and under what conditions do their international spillovers provoke retaliatory responses from trade partners? <br>_Rotunno, L., & Ruta, M. (2024): Trade Spillovers of Domestic Subsidies. IMF Working Paper._ |
| 5. | **Standards as Protection**<br>Consumer and environmental standards are often presented as mere regulations, but they can also serve as tools for protectionism under the guise of public interest.<br>Are consumer protection standards sometimes used as political cover for trade restrictions?<br>_Leonardi, M. & Meschi, E. (2024): Non-Tariff Barriers to Trade. The Journal of Law, Economics, and Organization._ |
| 6. | **NTBs and Firm Heterogeneity**<br>The costs of complying with complex regulatory standards may weigh more heavily on smaller exporters, while multinational firms often have the capacity to adjust.<br>To what extent do NTBs disadvantage small exporters more than multinational firms?<br>_Schmidt, J., & Steingress, W. (2022): No double standards: Quantifying the impact of standard harmonization on trade. JIE._ |
| 7. | **Technological Progress and Trade Flows**<br>Artificial intelligence has emerged as a disruptive technological innovation and is increasingly being adopted as a tool to reduce information frictions and transaction costs in international trade.<br>Have disruptive innovations increased trade flows and does that also hold for AI?<br>_Jakubik, A., Rotunno, L., Saini, A. (2025): Foresee the unseen: Evaluating the impact of artificial intelligence on international trade. Journal of Policy Modeling._ |
| 8. | **AI, Trade and Power**<br>Artificial intelligence is transforming the global economy, yet international trade rules are struggling to keep pace. As governments and tech giants compete to shape the digital trade agenda, emerging rules increasingly reflect commercial interests, while overlooking critical issues like algorithmic accountability, ethical standards, and labor market impacts.<br>How will AI affect international trade policy? <br>_Jones, E. (2023): Digital Disruption: Artificial Intelligence and International Trade Policy. Oxford Review of Economic Policy._ |
| 9. | **Firm-level linkages and business cycle synchronization**<br>International business-cycle comovement is often attributed to macroeconomic factors like interest rates, fiscal policy, and common shocks. However, new evidence suggests that firm-level international linkages may play a much larger role than previously thought.<br>Can the behavior of individual firms help explain why national economies move together?<br>_di Giovanni, J., Levchenko, A. A., Mejean, I. (2018): The micro origins of international business-cycle comovement. American Economic Review._ |
| 10. | **The microstructure of trade**<br>International trade is often viewed through the lens of country-level flows and aggregate statistics. Yet, behind these macro patterns lies a complex web of firm-to-firm connections, where a few large firms dominate transactions and form intricate production networks.<br>How do firm-to-firm networks influence the structure and dynamics of international trade flows?<br>_Bernard, A. B., & Moxnes, A. (2018): Networks and Trade. Annual Review of Economics._ |
