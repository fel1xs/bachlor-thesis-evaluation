## Course description

This course aims to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of how governments design and implement policies to influence economic activity. Students will examine key policy areas, including fiscal and monetary policy, exchange rate policy, international trade and environmental policy. Emphasis will be placed on the interplay between economic theory and real-world decision-making, considering constraints such as political feasibility, institutional frameworks, and global economic interdependencies. Topics include (but are not limited to):

1\. Market failure and public intervention

2\. Policy evaluation

3\. Expectations and time inconsistency of optimal policies

4\. Trade and the challenges for climate policies

5\. Keynesian demand stimulus

6\. Budget, deficit, and debt sustainability

7\. EMS crisis

## Course objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- analyze market failures using microeconomic policy frameworks
- explain the role of expectations in economic policy
- assess fiscal policy instruments
- analyze international policy interactions
- interpret historical policy episodes (e.g., exchange rate crises) using theoretical economic policy models
