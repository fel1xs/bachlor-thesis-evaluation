The seminar takes places in this winter semester.

## General Information

Advances in information and communication technologies, together with continued trade liberalization, have transformed global production over the past few decades. Production processes became increasingly vertically fragmented and geographically dispersed. This seminar introduces students to the concept of trade in tasks and examines how the emergence of global value chains shapes economic policymaking, addressing a range of different topics within this central theme. This course is also available on [Moodle](https://moodle2.uni-leipzig.de/course/view.php?id=55875 "Link öffnet in einem neuen Tab").

## Format and schedule

The seminar will be held as a 2–3 (half) day course in December 2025, depending on the number of participants. A kick-off meeting is scheduled for October 13, 2025, where we will discuss organizational details, finalize the seminar dates, and discuss the topics available for seminar papers. After the allocation of the topics, participants are encouraged to submit an exposé of their seminar project. The exposé (approx. 2–3 pages) should clarify the research question and motivation of the study, describe expected findings, and should include a (preliminary) table of contents and a list of references. It serves as the primary means of obtaining feedback from the supervisor prior to the presentation and submission of the final paper. Individual feedback on the exposés will be provided in the week after their submission.

## Seminar Requirements

Each student is required to write a seminar paper of approximately 12 pages (±10%), and to give two presentations: a 20-minute oral presentation on their chosen topic, and a 5-minute discussion of another topic, which will be randomly assigned from those presented by other seminar participants.

## Topics

1) Metrics and methods for tracing global value chains

2) Countries’ integration into global value chains

3) Industrial policy and strategic interventions

4) Carbon leakage and the effectiveness of climate policies

5) Transmission of price shocks in networked production structures

6) Supply chain resilience and diversification strategies

7) Trade policy in global value chains
