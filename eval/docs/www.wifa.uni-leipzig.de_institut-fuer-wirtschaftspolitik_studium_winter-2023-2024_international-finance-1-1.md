## Course Description

This course explores the functioning of foreign exchange (FX) markets and the determination of exchange rates from a finance-oriented perspective. It critically evaluates the limitations of traditional macroeconomic models in explaining real-world exchange rate behavior. The course highlights the central roles of investor expectations, speculative activity, and bounded rationality in driving market outcomes. Key topics include excess volatility, the mechanics of currency crises, and the effectiveness of political and policy interventions aimed at stabilizing exchange rates.

## Course Outline

1\. Introduction to Foreign Exchange Markets

• Structure, participants, and instruments of FX markets

• Overview of spot, forward, swap, and options markets

2\. Exchange Rate Determination and Macroeconomic Theory

• Key macroeconomic models (PPP, interest parity, monetary and portfolio balance)

• Exchange rate forecasting and market efficiency

3\. Speculation, Volatility, and Stabilization

• Market behavior and speculative dynamics

• Role of policy tools (e.g. Tobin Tax, target zones)

4\. Exchange Rate Crises and Speculative Attacks

• Models of currency crises: fundamentals, expectations, and financial fragility

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

• Better understand the structure and trading mechanisms of international FX markets

• Evaluate the shortcomings of standard macroeconomic models in explaining exchange rate movements

• Analyze the role of speculation and investor behavior in driving excess volatility

• Examine policy tools aimed at stabilizing exchange rates

• Understand theoretical models of currency crises and apply them to historical events
