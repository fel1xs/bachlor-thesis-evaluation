## Seminar Topics

**The Dynamics of Monetary Policy, Asset Prices and Consumer Prices**

When the Federal Reserve hiked rates in 2022, global equity and housing markets dropped almost instantly while consumer price inflation eased only slowly, reviving debate on how monetary shocks spill heterogeneously across asset and consumer prices. Most economists today hold that asset prices move in a separate sphere, with bubbles contained to financial markets and little pass-through to goods beyond luxury consumption. Others argue that ignoring overhang effects risks underestimating how changes in asset prices can transmit to the wider economy through credit allocation, wealth effects, or abrupt shifts in capital flows. This seminar looks at the dynamics between monetary policy, asset prices bubbles and consumer price inflation. It revisits the theory of monetary overhang to understand whether asset price bubbles such as those observed in cryptocurrencies or real estate matter for inflation dynamics, or whether they can be treated as isolated bubbles whose collapse leaves consumer prices largely untouched.

(1978). Macroeconomic models with quantity rationing. Economic Journal 88, no. 352, pp. 788-821._ |
| 2. | **Monetary overhang in the Soviet collapse**<br>In the Soviet collapse, suppressed consumer prices and excess ruble balances translated into runaway inflation once markets liberalized.<br>_Kornai, J. (1980). Economics of Shortage. North-Holland._ |
| 3. | **Japan’s lost decade: tightening, bubble burst, and deflation**<br>Japan’s 1985–2003 period shows how tight policy and a burst asset bubble can feed prolonged stagnation and deflation and so became the counterexample to the idea that monetary overhang inevitably leads to runaway inflation.<br>_Ito, T, and Mishkin, F. S. (2006). Two decades of Japanese monetary policy and the deflation problem. In Monetary Policy with Very Low Inflation in the Pacific Rim, NBER East Asia Seminar on Economics, vol. 15. University of Chicago Press._ |
| 4. | **Credit channel, asset price bubbles, and CPI**<br>The credit channel links asset price swings to consumer inflation through borrowing costs, financial markets, and lending behavior.<br>_Bernanke, B. S., and Gertler, M. (1999). Monetary Policy and Asset Price Volatility. Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City Economic Review._ |
| 5. | **Crypto wealth shocks and real spending**<br>Sudden gains or losses in crypto wealth can spill into real spending patterns, especially among younger cohorts.<br>_Aiello, D., Baker, S. R., Balyuk, T., Di Maggio, M., Johnson, M. J., & Kotter, J. D. (2023). The effects of cryptocurrency wealth on household consumption and investment (No. w31445). National Bureau of Economic Research._ |
| 6. | **US rate shocks, the global financial cycle, and imported inflation**<br>US rate shocks ripple through the global financial cycle, raising borrowing costs abroad and sometimes importing inflation into other economies.<br>_Rey, H. (2013). Dilemma not trilemma: The global financial cycle and monetary policy independence. Proceedings - Economic Policy Symposium - Jackson Hole, Federal Reserve of Kansas City Economic Symposium, p 285-333._ |
| 7. | **Should central banks react to excess liquidity?**<br>Central banks usually target consumer price inflation, but excess liquidity, money and credit beyond what the economy can absorb, can distort asset markets, feed speculative cycles, and create vulnerabilities well before CPI moves. <br>_Borio, C. E. V. and Lowe, P. W. (2002). Asset Prices, Financial and Monetary Stability: Exploring the Nexus. BIS Working Paper No. 114._ |
| 8. | **Monetary overhang in the QE–QT era**<br>Rapid shifts between monetary expansion and contraction raise questions about overhang effects on both assets and goods markets.<br>_Du, W., Forbes, K. J., & Luzzetti, M. (2024). Quantitative tightening around the globe: What have we learned?. NBER Working Paper, (w32321)._ |
| 9. | **Understanding the “price puzzle”: monetary phenomenon or econometric artifact?**<br>The “price puzzle” captures the counterintuitive rise in inflation after rate hikes, debated as either a real phenomenon or an econometric mirage.<br>_Sims, C. A. (1992). Interpreting the macroeconomic time series facts: The effects of monetary policy. European Economic Review 36, no. 5, pp. 975–1000._ |
| 10. | **Liquidity, inequality, and the value of money**<br>Excess liquidity amplifies wealth disparities as households channel resources into visible consumption and asset markets, which heightens social divisions and erodes trust. When confidence in money weakens under such conditions, inflationary pressure and the risk of disorder increase.<br>_Mian, A., Straub, L., & Sufi, A. (2021). Indebted demand. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 136(4), 2243-2307._ |
