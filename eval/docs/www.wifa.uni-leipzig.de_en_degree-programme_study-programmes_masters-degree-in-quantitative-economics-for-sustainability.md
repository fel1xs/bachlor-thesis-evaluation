How can the economy be transformed in ways that preserve our natural resources and bridge ecological sustainability and social justice? This is the central question addressed by the MSc Quantitative Economics for Sustainability (QuESt) at Leipzig University. The programme is aimed for students who want to analyse the sustainability transition of the economy in concrete terms and help shape it through economic expertise and quantitative methods. As an economics master’s programme with a clear focus on climate, biodiversity and natural resources, QuESt combines data-based analysis with economic modelling and integrated economic-natural science modelling. Students learn to assess transformation processes, analyse trade-offs, and develop a sound basis for decision-making in policy, public administration and practice.

## MSc Quantitative Economics for Sustainability (QuESt)

The MSc Quantitative Economics for Sustainability (QuESt) at Leipzig University is a full economics programme centred on the sustainable economy. Using quantitative methods from economics and drawing on interdisciplinary links with the natural sciences, QuESt explores how private and public economic activity can be organised in ways that preserve the natural foundations of human life and bring ecological sustainability together with social justice. As sustainability is increasingly understood as being quantitatively measurable, for example in relation to the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and their 169 targets, the focus is on quantitative methods.

At the heart of the programme are:

- statistical and econometric methods for processing and analysing data from observations and experiments
- economic and integrated economic-natural science modelling to examine the consequences of different courses of action for climate, biodiversity and resources

The programme is shaped by modules such as Climate Economics, Biodiversity Economics, Environmental Economics and Institutional Economics of Sustainability Transitions. At the same time, QuESt has an interdisciplinary profile and is broadened through elective options at the interface with climate and biodiversity science.

Students with very strong academic performance may spend part of their studies at Keio University in Tokyo or at Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne.

## Programme structure

**First semester (30 ECTS)**

- Quantitative Sustainability Economics (10 ECTS)
- Advanced Microeconomics (10 ECTS)
- Advanced Econometrics and Statistics (10 ECTS)

**Second semester (30 ECTS)**

- Advanced Macroeconomics (10 ECTS)
- two compulsory elective modules (10 ECTS each)

**Third semester (30 ECTS)**

- elective area or semester abroad (30 ECTS in total)

**Fourth semester (30 ECTS)**

- Research Methods in Sustainability Economics (10 ECTS)
- Master’s thesis (20 ECTS)

Overall, the programme includes 50 ECTS credits of compulsory modules, 20 ECTS credits of compulsory elective modules, 30 ECTS credits in the elective area, and a Master’s thesis worth 20 ECTS credits. Within the elective area, students can deepen their studies and broaden them through interdisciplinary options. Depending on their interests, they may choose modules offered by the Faculty of Economics and Management Science and other faculties at Leipzig University.

**Compulsory elective area**

Students must choose at least 20 ECTS credits from the following modules:

- Climate Economics (10 ECTS)
- Biodiversity Economics (10 ECTS)
- Environmental Economics (10 ECTS)
- Institutional Economics of Sustainability Transitions (10 ECTS)

**Elective area**

The elective area also includes further options such as:

- Ecological-Economic Modelling for the Sustainable Use and Conservation of Biodiversity (10 ECTS)
- interdisciplinary modules on climate change and/or biodiversity change
- elective modules from the MSc in Economics
- modules from Management Science, Business Information Systems, and Business Education and Management Training
- further modules offered by other faculties at Leipzig University

In addition, the [posters](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/fileadmin/Fakult%C3%A4t_Wifa/Institut_f%C3%BCr_Theoretische_Volkswirtschaftslehre/Professur_Makro%C3%B6konomik/Neue_VWL_Master_Webseiten/Poster_combined_compressed.pdf) for selected modules offer further insight into the content, teaching formats and academic focus of individual courses.

### [Career Prospects](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/degree-programme/study-programmes/masters-degree-in-quantitative-economics-for-sustainability\#collapse991573)

The Master's programme in Quantitative Economics for Sustainability (QuESt) equips students with strong analytical and quantitative skills for professional paths in the field of sustainability transitions. Using economic theory, empirical methods and model-based analysis, students learn how to examine economic, ecological and social relationships. They also develop the ability to assess transformation processes and to provide a sound basis for decision-making towards an ecologically and socially sustainable economy. At the centre of the programme is the question of how private and public economic activity can be organised in ways that preserve the natural foundations of life and strive for intra- and intergenerational justice.

For graduates with a research interest, the programme also opens the way to an academic career. It provides a good basis for doctoral study, for example in sustainability economics and related interdisciplinary fields such as the [Research Training Group Economics of Connected Natural Commons: Atmosphere and Biodiversity (ECO-N)](https://www.uni-leipzig.de/en/economics-of-connected-natural-commons). QuESt also prepares students for work at universities and research institutes concerned with the ecological and social transformation of the economy and society, including institutions such as [Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research (UFZ)](https://www.ufz.de/index.php?en=33573), [German Centre for Integrative Biodiversity Research (iDiv)](https://www.idiv.de/) [UFZ](https://www.ufz.de/), [iDiv](https://www.idiv.de/), or [Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research (PIK)](https://www.pik-potsdam.de/en/home).

Beyond academia, graduates can pursue a wide range of career paths, for example in ministries, environmental agencies such as [German Environment Agency (UBA)](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/en) or [Federal Agency for Nature Conservation (BfN)](https://www.bfn.de/en), international organisations, Non-Governmental Organizations, corporate sustainability departments, and data- or risk-focused roles in financial institutions. The programme therefore opens up professional opportunities in fields such as climate and environmental policy, biodiversity conservation, sustainability management, transformation research, and evidence-based public decision-making.

### [Admission requirements](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/degree-programme/study-programmes/masters-degree-in-quantitative-economics-for-sustainability\#collapse991575)

Students applying for the Master's Programme in Quantitative Economics for Sustainability (QuESt) should have a strong interest in current questions surrounding the sustainability transformation of the economy and society, social justice, and academic work. They are expected to bring solid foundations in mathematics, microeconomics, macroeconomics, econometrics and statistics, together with a willingness to engage closely with statistical and econometric methods for analysing data from observations and experiments. Equally important is an openness to working with economic models and integrated economic-natural science models to assess the consequences of different courses of action for climate, biodiversity and resources. Strong analytical and abstract thinking skills, logical reasoning, and a confident standard of written and spoken expression are also important.

**Admission requirements**

To apply for the MSc QuESt, you need:

- English language skills at B2 level of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages
- a certificate of a first higher education degree in economics or a related business field, including a Transcript of Records and Diploma Supplement, or, if the degree can be completed before the start of the master’s programme, proof of academic achievements completed to date

**Selection criteria**

Applications are assessed primarily on the basis of:

- previous academic performance, such as GPA or average mark
- academic background and overall fit with the programme

**Language requirements**

The language of instruction is English, and the programme can be completed entirely in English. All compulsory and compulsory elective modules are taught in English. The majority of optional modules are also offered in English, while a small number are available in German.

English proficiency at B2 level can, for example, be demonstrated by a German Abitur certificate including English from Years 7 to 12 (or 13). Other recognised proofs include standardised language tests such as TOEFL iBT, FCE, IELTS, TOEIC, Linguaskill and UNIcert.

The Faculty reviews whether the academic and language requirements have been met. The selection committee then issues a corresponding decision.

## FAQ: Frequently Asked Questions

### [Questions about applying](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/degree-programme/study-programmes/masters-degree-in-quantitative-economics-for-sustainability\#collapse991594)

## What are the admission requirements for the Master's degree programme?

The general qualification for the study program is proven by a first higher education degree qualifying for entry to a profession. The additionally subject-specific admission requirements are:

- a completed Bachelor’s degree with predominantly business as well as economics content or a comparable degree (i.e. also from a university of applied sciences) or
- Applicants must be proficient in written and spoken English according to level B2. German language proficiency is not an admission requirement.

## Is there an aptitude test?

No. There is no separate aptitude test and no selection interviews. All applicants take part in the selection procedure.

## Which documents do I need to submit for the selection procedure?

The required documents must be submitted on time together with the application for participation in the selection procedure. These include:

- proof of higher education entrance qualification,
- a certificate of a first professionally qualifying higher education degree, including the Transcript of Records and Diploma Supplement, or proof of the academic achievements completed so far if the degree can be obtained before the start of the master’s programme,
- where applicable, evidence of degree-related vocational training, voluntary internships, or similar activities,
- a letter of motivation.

## How does the selection process work?

Admission is based on a selection procedure. Five criteria are assessed, each contributing between 0 and 30 points to the overall score. A maximum of 150 points can therefore be achieved in total.

The following criteria are taken into account in the selection procedure:

- the suitability of the documented professionally qualifying higher education degree for the objectives of the master’s programme, as evidenced in the Diploma Supplement,
- the final grade of the higher education degree,
- the ECTS volume of quantitative and mathematical modules in economics, namely (1) microeconomics, (2) macroeconomics, and (3) statistics and econometrics,
- the grades achieved in quantitative and mathematical modules in economics, namely (1) microeconomics, (2) macroeconomics, and (3) statistics and econometrics,
- the letter of motivation.

## How are applicants selected?

Applicants are ranked according to their total score. A higher score leads to a better ranking. In the event of a tie, the final decision is made by lot.

## Is the Master's degree programme admission restricted?

Yes, the MSc Quantitative Economics for Sustainability is admission restricted.

## What language skills are required for the Master's degree programme?

Applicants must be proficient in written and spoken English according to level B2 of the "Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, Teaching and Assessment". English proficiency at B2 level can, for example, be demonstrated by a German Abitur certificate including English from Years 7 to 12 (or 13). Applications are submitted through the Faculty’s application portal as part of the regular application round for Erasmus+ study-abroad periods. In the current application cycle, the deadline is always 1 December, two semesters before departure. For example, if students wish to go abroad in the winter semester 2027/28, they must apply by 1 December 2026. The current deadlines and guidance issued by the Faculty are decisive.

## Which documents are required for the application?

Applicants must submit the documents required in the application portal. Depending on the programme, additional documents such as a CV and language certificates may be required.

## Which language requirements apply?

The language requirements depend on the respective partner university. At present, English at B2 level is required both for the Sorbonne and for Keio University. Regardless of this, the current requirements of the relevant partner university should always be checked again before applying.

## How are places allocated?

Places are allocated through a structured selection process. This may take into account, among other things, academic performance, semester standing, methodological background, and motivation. Some programmes may also have additional programme-specific selection criteria.

## When will I find out whether I have been offered a place?

The outcome of the selection process is usually communicated in January.

## What options do I have to make up for the basic knowledge in the subject Microeconomics?

Self-study of the contents of a bachelor-level textbook such as the following, which is used in the BSc Economics and Management Science at Leipzig University and is freely available online via the university library:

Varian, Hal R. _Grundzüge der Mikroökonomik_. Berlin/Boston: De Gruyter Oldenbourg, 2016. [https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110478051](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110478051)

Essential requirements include, in particular, basic mathematical knowledge of calculus. The mathematical appendix of the textbook listed above covers only the absolute minimum and is assumed knowledge. In addition, applicants should have the following basic knowledge of microeconomics: individual demand in goods markets as the solution to utility maximisation subject to a budget constraint, and, analogously, supply as the solution to profit maximisation subject to a technology constraint, in both cases including an economic interpretation of the relevant optimality conditions; market demand and market supply; simple market equilibrium analysis using comparative statics; discounting; expected utility; static games, strategies, Nash equilibrium, and the strategic interpretation of Nash equilibrium.

## What possibilities do I have to catch up on the basic knowledge in the subject Macroeconomics?

The Bachelor lecture Macroeconomics takes place in the winter term and can be attended if necessary with the approval of the responsible lecturer; however, it is not possible to acquire credit points here. You can then attend the regular master's course in the second term. If you have further questions, you can also contact the [Chair of Macroeconomics](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-theoretische-vwl/chairs/macroeconomics) directly.

As literature for a self-study of the basics of macroeconomics we recommend:

Burda, M. and C. Wyplosz (2009), Macroeconomics - A European Text, Oxford University Press: Oxford, 5th edition.

## What options do I have to catch up on the basic knowledge in statistics?

The Bachelor lecture Statistics takes place in the winter term and can be attended, if necessary, with the approval of the responsible lecturer; however, it is not possible to acquire credit points here. Please note that the subjects Statistics and Econometrics are only examined together in the Master's degree programme! If you have further questions, you can also contact the [Professorship of Statistics](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institute-for-empirical-economic-research/statistics) directly.

As literature for a self-study of the basics of statistics we recommend:

Fahrmeir, L., R. Künstler, I. Pigeot and G. Tutz (2009), Statistics - The way to data analysis, Springer: Heidelberg, New York, 7th edition.

## What options do I have to catch up on the basic knowledge in the subject Econometrics?

If applicants do not yet have the relevant prior knowledge, they should work through it independently before starting the programme.

Particularly important are foundations in statistical inference, simple and multiple OLS regression, statistical testing, and the interpretation of standard regression output. Students should also have a basic understanding of possible violations of assumptions and their consequences. Some familiarity with more advanced methods, such as instrumental variable and probit models, as well as time series analysis, is also helpful.

We recommend studying by topic and, where possible, using more than one textbook. Particularly accessible introductions include, for example:

- Hill, R. C., W. E. Griffiths und G. C. Lim, _Principles of Econometrics_, Wiley.

[https://principlesofeconometrics.com/](https://principlesofeconometrics.com/)
- Wooldridge, J. M., _Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach_, Cengage.

[https://faculty.cengage.com/works/9780357900161](https://faculty.cengage.com/works/9780357900161)
- Baum, C. F., _An Introduction to Modern Econometrics Using Stata_, Stata Press.

[https://www.stata.com/bookstore/modern-econometrics-stata/](https://www.stata.com/bookstore/modern-econometrics-stata/)
- Acock, A. C., _A Gentle Introduction to Stata_, Stata Press.

[https://www.stata.com/bookstore/microeconometrics-stata/](https://www.stata.com/bookstore/microeconometrics-stata/)
- Baum, C. F., _An Introduction to Time Series Analysis Using Stata_, Stata Press.

[https://www.stata.com/bookstore/introduction-to-time-series-using-stata/](https://www.stata.com/bookstore/introduction-to-time-series-using-stata/)

In addition, we expect students to be able to work with statistical software and interpret its output. Familiarity with at least one statistical package, such as Stata or R, is recommended.

## Is it possible to take semesters off during the Master's degree program for internships or similar?

Yes, this is possible. Up to two [semesters of leave](https://www.uni-leipzig.de/en/studium/im-studium/modification-and-change/leave-of-absence) can be taken during the Master's degree programme.
