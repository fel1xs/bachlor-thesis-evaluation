The Joint International Master's Program in Sustainable Development is an offer of the University of Leipzig together with the University of Utrecht (Netherlands), the Ca' Foscari University of Venice (Italy) and the Karl-Franzens-University of Graz (Austria) as program coordinator.

The universities of Basel (Switzerland), Hiroshima (Japan) and TERI University (India) are also involved.

## Inter- and Transdisciplinary Teaching for Sustainability

The Master's program enables participants to make substantial scientific contributions to societies’ transition toward sustainability. To this end, it focuses on sustainability-related research and intervention strategies and fosters skills in inter- and transdisciplinary scientific work.

The programme prepares students for doctoral studies and a research-oriented career. Furthermore,  its social orientation is a good starting point for positions that require a scientific qualification but are not directly research-related.

[Information about the international program and the application procedure](https://www.jointdegree.eu/de/sustainable-development/ "Link öffnet in einem neuen Tab")

## Study Program

### [1st Semester: Winter Semester](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/wassermanagement-und-klimaanpassung/lehre/joint-international-master-in-sustainable-development\#collapse529497)

### Basics in Sustainable Development (10 ECTS)

- “Theories of Sustainability”

The lecture reflects on sustainability as a political, scientific and ethical concept and presents selected implementation approaches and theories.

-  “Integrated Assessment of Climate Proctection Strategies”

The course aims to provide a coherent mathematical framework for analyzing the interactions between socio-economic development and climate change.

-  “Selected Topics of Corporate Sustainability Management”

Using the example of current topics in corporate sustainability management, the seminar examines the question of how economic goals can be reconciled with ecological and social requirements in a corporate context.

### Basics in Economic Sciences (10 ECTS)

-  “Sustainable Economics I and II”

The course introduces the basics of micro- and macroeconomics. The effects of economic activity on sustainability are dealt with in depth.

-  “Transformation Economics”

Central economic instruments of environmental and/or climate policy are presented and their effects are analyzed. The role of technological and economic innovations in decoupling economic growth and the associated environmental impacts is explained and reflected upon.

### Introduction to Social Sciences (10 ECTS)

- “International Studies”

The module introduces different approaches from the humanities and social sciences to the discussion of globalization processes and development policy. The focus is on theories and approaches from the political science sub-field of international relations, development economics, new urban sociology, global history and new political geography.

### Environmental & Resources Economics

- “Sustainable Energy Economics” (10 ECTS)

Participants will be able to understand the functioning of energy markets - especially liberalized energy markets - and evaluate the suitability of the different approaches used today for national and international energy-related decision-making processes. Furthermore, they learn to develop simple models and to select suitable solution methods for the most important decision problems in these areas.

[Further Information: Chair of Energy Management and Sustainability](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/professur-fuer-energiemanagement-und-nachhaltigkeit/study)

- “Biodiversity Economics” (10 ECTS)

Students are able to use environmental economic concepts and methods to evaluate and critically scrutinize current environmental and biodiversity policies with regard to macroeconomic efficiency and distributional effects. They are able to independently apply environmental economic approaches in order to develop well-founded proposals for solutions to environmental problems and biodiversity loss.

- “Climate Economics” (10 ECTS)

Students are able to describe and critically evaluate the causes and effects of anthropogenic climate change. They are able to use climate economic concepts and methods to incorporate climate change into economic models and to evaluate and critically analyze policy instruments to prevent climate change with regard to macroeconomic efficiency and distributional effects.

[Further Information: Chair of Nature’s Values](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/natures-values/teaching)

### Environmental & Resources Management

- “Energy Engineering and Management” (10 ECTS)

The aim of this module is to provide a solid basic knowledge of the technological, environmental and economic aspects of different energy technologies. Students will acquire the necessary skills to evaluate the comparative advantages of different energy supply options at the operational level.

[Further Information: Chair of Energy Management and Sustainability](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/professur-fuer-energiemanagement-und-nachhaltigkeit/study)

- “Water Resource Management” (10 ECTS)

Using freshwater as an example, this course explores the question of by whom and with which instruments water resources can best be managed. Characteristic resource conflicts are highlighted, an overview of valuation issues and valuation procedures is provided, the importance of applying forecasting tools is addressed, and possible decision criteria for related conflict situations are discussed.

[Further Information: Chair of Water Management and Climate Adaption](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/umwelttechnik-in-der-wasserwirtschaft/lehre)

- “Landscape Management” (5 ECTS)

The module enables students to explain the multifunctionality of landscapes and their land uses, the strategies and approaches for sustainable land management and the assessment of the landscape in the context of sustainability and climate change from an economic, ecological and social perspective. They will be able to recognize land use conflicts and identify key similarities and differences in the context of land use decisions based on risk analyses within a European framework.

- “Urban Land Management” (5 ECTS)

The module teaches students to recognize the different functions of land use in urban areas and to evaluate them with regard to sustainability criteria. They are able to identify land use conflicts in urban areas and are familiar with strategies and approaches for sustainable land management. Building on this, students are able to independently apply regulatory, planning, economic and informational management instruments.

### [3rd Semester: Winter Semester](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/wassermanagement-und-klimaanpassung/lehre/joint-international-master-in-sustainable-development\#collapse529479)

### Inter- or Transdisciplinary Case Study (15 ECTS)

This research-oriented module is designed to enable students to independently analyze and solve complex and conflictual sub-questions from a selected area of sustainable development in a multidisciplinary scientific approach.

### Project Management and Communication Skills (5 ECTS)

The module teaches standard project management tools as well as communication strategies and models to effectively promote transformation and change within an organization.

### Electives (10 ECTS)

- Life Cycle Analyses and Sustainability Assessment of Bioeconomy (5 ECTS)

After participating in the module, the students are able to assess ecological, economic and social aspects of renewable production systems, especially bioeconomy systems. They are familiar with concepts of sustainability, renewable raw materials, energy and material flow management and their practical modeling and application. This enables them to independently apply life cycle analysis methods for processes, products, production systems and services, to scientifically interpret and discuss the qualitative and quantitative results and to defend them argumentatively.

[Further Information: Chair of Bioenergy System](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/chair-of-bioenergy-systems/education)

- Natural Resource Use and Conservation Economics (10 ECTS)

The module teaches the main methods and concepts of modern resource and sustainability economics to calculate the optimal use of natural resources (e.g., forests, fisheries, biodiversity) and their conservation for future generations.

- Modelling Resources Management (10 ECTS)

Students will be able to describe the main approaches to modeling energy systems and will be able to program simple computer models to analyze energy management and policy issues. They will also receive an introduction to agent-based modeling, which is becoming increasingly important in the field of sustainability management.

[Further Information: Chair of Energy Management and Sustainability](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/professur-fuer-energiemanagement-und-nachhaltigkeit/study)

- Socio-Economic Aspects of Urban Development (10 ECTS)

Cities face challenges as they grow. What are the needs and demands of residents as well as business executives to keep cities thriving, and how can public policy respond? We will be analyzing cities quantitatively, looking at income differences within cities, social segregration, business agglomeration and infrastructure connections. Examples from cities around the world will illustrate how climate change affects urban development and how cities respond to the challenges of the future.

[Further Information: Chair of Sustainable Real Estate and Urban Development](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/nachhaltige-immobilien-und-stadtentwicklung#c859942)

- Entrepreneurship Management (10 ECTS)

Students are able to independently develop and design business models and plans and plan their implementation. To this end, they use the latest concepts of business model and business plan development and are able to apply these in real start-up projects.

### Master Thesis (30 ECTS)

The master's thesis, with a workload of 30 ECTS, is usually written in the fourth semester. The processing time is 23 weeks. The thesis is assessed by two examiners: one teacher from the program at Leipzig University and one from a partner university (usually the student's mobility university).

[Further Information about thesis](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/umwelttechnik-in-der-wasserwirtschaft/lehre#c932765 "Link öffnet in einem neuen Tab")

[Study office](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/degree-programme/organisation-of-studies/examination-matters "Link öffnet in einem neuen Tab")

### Electives (alternative to 3rd semester)

- Big Data and Smart Cities (10 ECTS)

Data is everywhere - and the amount available is growing by the second. At the same time, our world is becoming more urbanized.

In this course, students will learn how to gather, process and analyze (big) data. They will get know statistical methods to draw insights from (big) data, discover patterns and relationships, and make predictions for urban life. In a nutshell, the question to be answered is: How we use (big) data in a responsible way to make cities more efficient and sustainable?

[Further Information: Chair of Sustainable Real Estate and Urban Development](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-fuer-infrastruktur-und-ressourcenmanagement/professuren/nachhaltige-immobilien-und-stadtentwicklung#c859942)

- Sustainable Finance (5 ECTS)

After participation in the module, students will be able to describe the characteristics of sustainable investments and to classify them in the landscape of complete capital markets. Furthermore, students will be able to independently identify the particular current opportunities and risks of using sustainable financial products and determine the resulting risk-return profiles for investors. Students are able to critically analyze current regulatory and political challenges in the context of sustainable financial services, and to reflect on and directly apply approaches to solutions in new issues using scientific methods.

[Further Information: Chair of Sustainable Banking and Finance](https://www.wifa.uni-leipzig.de/en/institut-for-commerce-and-banking/chair-of-sustainable-banking-and-finance/study/master)
